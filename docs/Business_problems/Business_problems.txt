1. Default Risk Scoring at the Point of Application

Business question: "Given only what we know at the moment someone applies, how likely are they to default?"

This is the core problem — using application_train's demographic, income, employment, and property fields (plus bureau history merged in) to produce a probability score per applicant. This directly answers the loan officer's real-time question: approve, decline, or price the risk. It's also your primary ML deliverable (Phase 4) and the Streamlit app (Phase 6).


2. Early Warning Signals from External Credit Behavior

Business question: "Is this applicant already showing signs of financial distress with other lenders?"

bureau.csv and bureau_balance.csv let you build features like number of active credit lines elsewhere, overdue amounts, and recent credit inquiries (AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_*). This answers a more specific business need than #1: detecting applicants who look fine on the surface (stable income, employed) but are already overextended elsewhere — a classic hidden-risk pattern banks care about.


3. Repayment Behavior Prediction from Prior Relationship History

Business question: "For returning customers, does their track record with us predict future default?"

previous_application.csv, installments_payments.csv, and POS_CASH_balance.csv/credit_card_balance.csv let you build features like late payment frequency, early repayment behavior, and how often past applications were refused. This answers a retention/relationship-based question — existing customers can often be risk-assessed more accurately than brand-new applicants, and it's a natural segment to highlight in your dashboard (new vs. returning applicant risk).


4. Portfolio-Level Risk Segmentation for Business Strategy

Business question: "Where is default risk concentrated across our loan book, and what's driving it?"

This is a dashboard/BI question, not just a model question — default rate by income band, occupation, loan type, age group, region. It answers what senior stakeholders (not underwriters) actually want: "should we tighten lending criteria for X segment?" or "which products/regions are dragging down portfolio performance?"


5. Optimal Loan Terms / Affordability Fit

Business question: "Is this applicant being offered a loan amount and repayment structure they can actually sustain?"

Using AMT_CREDIT, AMT_ANNUITY, AMT_INCOME_TOTAL, and repayment history from installments_payments.csv, you can construct debt-to-income and payment-to-income ratios and show how mismatched loan terms (large annuity relative to income) correlate with default. This is a more prescriptive/actionable business problem than pure risk scoring — it points toward how to restructure an offer to reduce risk, not just whether to approve it.

Here's the feature engineering checklist, organized by source table and mapped back to the business problems they serve.

1. From bureau.csv + bureau_balance.csv (serves Problem #2 — early warning from external credit)

Feature to engineer	-- What it captures

BUREAU_COUNT	-- Total number of credit lines at other institutions
BUREAU_ACTIVE_COUNT	-- Number of currently active external credit lines
BUREAU_OVERDUE_COUNT	-- Number of bureau credit lines with overdue payments
BUREAU_AVG_DAYS_CREDIT	-- Average age of external credit history (longer = more established)
BUREAU_MAX_OVERDUE_AMT	-- Largest overdue amount across external credit lines
BUREAU_CREDIT_TYPE_DIVERSITY	-- Number of distinct credit types held (credit card, car loan, mortgage, etc.)
BUREAU_BALANCE_WORST_STATUS	-- Worst monthly delinquency status seen in bureau_balance (0=current, 5=written off)

2. From previous_application.csv (serves Problem #3 — prior relationship history)

Feature to engineer	-- What it captures

PREV_APP_COUNT	-- Number of prior applications with Home Credit
PREV_APPROVED_COUNT / PREV_REFUSED_COUNT	-- Approval/refusal history — repeated refusals are a strong risk signal
PREV_APPROVAL_RATE	-- Ratio of approved to total previous applications
PREV_AVG_CREDIT_AMT	-- Average size of previously requested credit
PREV_CANCELLED_COUNT	-- Applications the client themselves cancelled (can signal indecision or improved offers elsewhere)


3. From installments_payments.csv (serves Problem #3 and Problem #5 — repayment behavior + affordability)

Feature to engineer	-- What it captures

INSTALLMENT_LATE_COUNT	-- Number of installments paid late
INSTALLMENT_LATE_RATIO	-- % of past installments paid late
INSTALLMENT_AVG_DAYS_LATE	-- Average number of days late when payments were late
INSTALLMENT_UNDERPAYMENT_COUNT	-- Number of times paid amount was less than scheduled amount
INSTALLMENT_PAYMENT_CONSISTENCY	-- Std. deviation of days-late (volatility, not just average)


4. From POS_CASH_balance.csv + credit_card_balance.csv (serves Problem #3 and Problem #5)

Feature to engineer	-- What it captures

POS_DPD_MAX	-- Max "days past due" seen on past POS/cash loans
CC_UTILIZATION_AVG	-- Average credit card balance relative to credit limit (classic risk indicator)
CC_MIN_PAYMENT_ONLY_COUNT	-- Number of months only the minimum payment was made (revolving debt risk)


5. From application_train.csv itself (serves Problem #1 and Problem #5 — already partly done in Phase 2)

Feature to engineer	-- What it captures

DEBT_TO_INCOME_RATIO	-- AMT_CREDIT / AMT_INCOME_TOTAL_CAPPED
ANNUITY_TO_INCOME_RATIO	AMT_ANNUITY / AMT_INCOME_TOTAL_CAPPED — the core affordability metric
CREDIT_TERM	-- AMT_CREDIT / AMT_ANNUITY — implied loan length in periods
EMPLOYMENT_TO_AGE_RATIO	DAYS_EMPLOYED / DAYS_BIRTH — employment stability relative to life stage
