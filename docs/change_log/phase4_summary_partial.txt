KEY FINDINGS SUMMARY
----------------------
Occupation:
    Highest risk: Low-skill Laborers, Drivers, Waiters/Barmen staff
    Lowest risk: Accountants, High-skill Tech staff
    Driver: income stability / job security, not occupation prestige
    alone

Age:
    Clean, monotonic decline in default risk as age increases
    (20s = highest risk, 60s = lowest)
    Caveat: age-based decisioning has fair-lending implications;
    should inform features/pricing, not blanket eligibility rules

Income band:
    Raw income level showed a misleading non-monotonic pattern
    (Medium band appeared riskiest). Root cause: "Very Low" income
    band is disproportionately pensioners (31.4% vs 9.6-18.4% in
    other bands) with stable guaranteed income, masking true risk.
    Once accounted for, ANNUITY_TO_INCOME_RATIO shows the real, clean
    risk gradient (24.0% of income at lowest tier down to 12.4% at
    highest)

Loan amount band:
    Also showed a misleading non-monotonic pattern, driven by TWO
    separate confounds: pensioner concentration at the low end
    (same effect as income), and implicit pre-existing creditworthiness
    screening at the high end (larger loans go to already-vetted,
    lower-risk applicants despite carrying a higher relative payment
    burden). Loan amount should not be read as a standalone causal
    risk driver.

Implied credit cost ratio (substitute for interest rate, which is not
present in the source data):
    Clean, largely monotonic increase in default risk as cost of
    credit relative to goods/loan value increases (6.7% at lowest to
    11.4% at highest), with one minor single-band irregularity noted
    but not further investigated

Region rating (substitute for geography, which is anonymized in the
source data):
    Clean, fully monotonic increase in default risk from best-rated
    (4.8%) to worst-rated (11.1%) regions. No confound found; most
    straightforwardly trustworthy chart of the six.


CROSS-CUTTING TAKEAWAYS
-------------------------
1. STABILITY over LEVEL: across occupation, age, and income, income/
   employment STABILITY consistently predicts risk better than raw
   LEVEL (income amount, job prestige, age alone).

2. Raw "amount" or "level" metrics are not always safe to interpret
   at face value: both income level and loan amount produced
   misleading non-monotonic patterns traceable to compositional
   confounds (pensioner concentration) or hidden selection effects
   (implicit underwriting pre-screening reflected in loan size).
   Ratio-based and directly risk-relevant metrics (ANNUITY_TO_INCOME_
   RATIO, IMPLIED_CREDIT_COST_RATIO, REGION_RATING_CLIENT) proved
   more reliable and should be favored as headline dashboard metrics.
