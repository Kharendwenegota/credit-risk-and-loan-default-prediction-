EDA ANALYSIS: LOAN AMOUNT, IMPLIED CREDIT COST, AND REGION RATING
Credit Risk & Loan Default Prediction Project — Phase 4

Charts covered in this analysis:
    4. Default Rate by Loan Amount Band
    5. Default Rate by Implied Credit Cost Ratio
    6. Default Rate by Region Rating


===========================================================
4. DEFAULT RATE BY LOAN AMOUNT (AMT_CREDIT)
===========================================================

OBSERVATION
-----------
Non-monotonic ("hump-shaped") pattern:
    Very Low   : 7.2%
    Low        : 9.2%
    Medium     : 10.0%  (peak)
    High       : 7.9%
    Very High  : 6.1%   (lowest)

This mirrors the same non-monotonic shape found in the income band
analysis (see phase4_eda_analysis_occupation_age_income.txt) and was
investigated using the same method: check for compositional
confounds before trusting the raw pattern.

INVESTIGATION
--------------
Pensioner share by loan amount band:
    Very Low   : 23.4%
    Low        : 17.8%
    Medium     : 17.0%
    High       : 17.4%
    Very High  : 13.9%

Annuity-to-income ratio by loan amount band:
    Very Low   : 0.124
    Low        : 0.153
    Medium     : 0.191
    High       : 0.208
    Very High  : 0.231

FINDING — TWO SEPARATE CONFOUNDS IDENTIFIED
---------------------------------------------
1) Low end (Very Low band): Nearly double the pensioner concentration
   of the Very High band (23.4% vs 13.9%). As established in the
   income analysis, pensioners carry low but stable, guaranteed
   income and are genuinely lower-risk — this artificially suppresses
   the observed default rate for the smallest loans, same masking
   effect seen with income.

2) High end (High / Very High bands): Annuity-to-income ratio rises
   smoothly and monotonically as loan amount increases — i.e. larger
   loans genuinely carry a proportionally larger repayment burden
   relative to income. Under a simple affordability model, this
   should INCREASE default risk. Instead, observed default risk
   DECREASES at these bands. This indicates a second, different
   confound: applicants who qualify for very large loans have likely
   already been pre-screened by Home Credit's own underwriting
   process (e.g. stronger EXT_SOURCE credit bureau scores, more
   stable employment, better collateral) — meaning loan size is not
   randomly assigned, but partly reflects prior assessed
   creditworthiness. This is functionally a data leakage risk if
   loan amount is read as a standalone causal risk driver.

CONCLUSION
----------
Loan amount, taken alone, is NOT a clean or reliable standalone risk
signal. It is confounded in opposite directions at each end of its
range: pensioner concentration suppresses risk at the low end, and
implicit pre-existing creditworthiness screening suppresses risk at
the high end.

DASHBOARD / BUSINESS IMPLICATION
----------------------------------
Do NOT present "loan amount vs default rate" as a standalone insight
without this caveat attached — in isolation it risks a dangerous
misreading (e.g. "larger loans default less, so we should approve
larger loans"), when the real driver is the applicant quality that
already determined loan eligibility, not the loan size itself.
Any model built on this data should treat loan amount as one input
among several correlated features (income, credit bureau scores,
employment stability) rather than a standalone lever.


===========================================================
5. DEFAULT RATE BY IMPLIED CREDIT COST RATIO
===========================================================

NOTE ON METRIC
---------------
Home Credit's dataset does not include an explicit interest rate
field. IMPLIED_CREDIT_COST_RATIO was engineered as a substitute:
    (AMT_CREDIT - AMT_GOODS_PRICE) / AMT_GOODS_PRICE
representing how much more the applicant is financing beyond the
cash price of the goods/loan purpose — a reasonable proxy for cost of
credit given the available fields. This substitution is noted
explicitly here and should be labeled clearly (not as literal
"interest rate") wherever it appears in the dashboard.

OBSERVATION
-----------
    Very Low   : 6.7%
    Low        : 4.7%   (dip, slightly below Very Low)
    Medium     : 6.7%
    High       : 9.5%
    Very High  : 11.4%  (highest)

INTERPRETATION
--------------
The overall trend is clean and intuitive: default risk rises sharply
as the implied cost of credit increases, with a clear jump between
Medium and High/Very High bands. This is the expected pattern — the
more expensive credit is relative to the underlying goods/loan
value, the higher the observed default risk.

One minor irregularity: the "Low" band sits slightly below "Very Low"
rather than continuing a smooth increase from the bottom. This is a
small, single-band deviation (not a full inverted pattern like the
loan amount chart) and was not investigated further, but should be
noted as a minor caveat rather than presented as a perfectly smooth
monotonic trend.

DASHBOARD / BUSINESS IMPLICATION
----------------------------------
This is a strong, largely trustworthy risk signal and a good
dashboard-ready chart. Cost of credit relative to loan purpose value
appears to be a genuine, monotonic (with one minor exception) driver
of default risk — supports pricing- and terms-based risk
segmentation.


===========================================================
6. DEFAULT RATE BY REGION RATING
===========================================================

NOTE ON METRIC
---------------
Home Credit's dataset anonymizes geography; there is no city/province
field. REGION_RATING_CLIENT (1=best, 3=worst) is Home Credit's own
anonymized region quality rating and is used here as the geographic
proxy. For dashboard purposes, this can be conceptually framed as an
urban/rural or provincial risk-tier analogy (consistent with this
project's South African market framing) but should be labeled clearly
as an anonymized proxy, not real geographic data.

OBSERVATION
-----------
    Rating 1 (best)  : 4.8%
    Rating 2          : 7.9%
    Rating 3 (worst)  : 11.1%

INTERPRETATION
--------------
Clean, fully monotonic increase in default risk as region rating
worsens. This matches intuition directly and required no further
investigation — no compositional confound was found or suspected.

DASHBOARD / BUSINESS IMPLICATION
----------------------------------
This is a clean, trustworthy, dashboard-ready finding. Region rating
is a strong standalone risk signal and supports geographic/regional
risk-based pricing or portfolio concentration analysis.


===========================================================
CROSS-CUTTING TAKEAWAY (UPDATED)
===========================================================
Combined with the earlier occupation/age/income analysis, a second
theme emerges: several seemingly simple "amount" or "level" metrics
(income level, loan amount) are NOT safe to interpret in isolation —
both showed non-monotonic patterns that trace back to compositional
confounds (pensioner concentration) or hidden selection effects
(implicit pre-screening reflected in loan size). By contrast, ratio-
based and directly risk-relevant metrics (ANNUITY_TO_INCOME_RATIO,
IMPLIED_CREDIT_COST_RATIO, REGION_RATING_CLIENT) showed clean,
trustworthy, largely monotonic relationships with default risk.

RECOMMENDATION FOR DASHBOARD DESIGN:
Favor ratio-based and directly risk-relevant features as headline
dashboard metrics. Where raw "level" metrics (income, loan amount)
are shown, pair them with the relevant confound caveat established
here, or avoid presenting them as standalone causal drivers.
